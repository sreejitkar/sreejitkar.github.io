The Inverted Stack: Silicon Moats, Habit Loops, and the Economics of Generative AI

September 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Every tech supercycle tells the same story: hardware gets commoditized, and software captures all the profit. In the cloud era, chipmakers kept barely 6% of gross profits while SaaS (hosted software) companies printed 80% gross margins—the share of revenue left after paying the direct costs of delivering the service.

When generative AI took off, everyone expected a rerun: foundation models would commoditize, compute costs would drop to zero, and application wrappers would capture the kingdom.

Except that didn’t happen. Recently, while studying the market dynamics inside Stanford’s MS&E 435 (The Economics of Generative AI, designed by Apoorv Agarwal) alongside my own low-level systems work, the data made one thing glaringly obvious: the generative AI stack is not flipping. It is stubbornly, radically inverted.

"In cloud, chipmakers make 6% of the profits and apps take 70%. In generative AI, chipmakers capture nearly 80% of all gross profits. Jensen Huang didn't just sell shovels in a gold rush; he bought the mountain."

1. The Inverted Pyramid ($435B of Revenue)

Over the past two years, the generative AI market expanded 5x to ~$435B in annualized revenue. But look at where the cash actually pools:

  • Semiconductors (~$300B): Overwhelmingly NVIDIA (~$250B datacenter run-rate), plus Broadcom’s custom accelerators (~$34B) and high-bandwidth memory. NVIDIA alone is ~80% of this tier.
  • Cloud Infra (~$75B): AWS, Azure, GCP, OCI, and CoreWeave—locked in a brutal capital race.
  • Applications (~$60B): OpenAI and Anthropic take ~$45B (75% of it). The rest is split between Cursor, Glean, ElevenLabs, and Perplexity.

The profit picture is even wilder: NVIDIA runs at ~73% gross margins. Cloud providers make ~55% (which sinks to 25–30% once you write down GPU depreciation). AI apps run at 30–50%.

Multiply it out: chipmakers capture ~$225B out of ~$285B in ecosystem gross profit (~79%). Over two years of breakneck growth, the application layer gained back barely 4 percentage points of profit share.

Why? Because AI breaks software physics. As I wrote in The Economics of Inference, running inference (generating answers from a trained model) isn't zero-marginal-cost code. Because autoregressive token generation is memory-bandwidth bound, the GPU spends 99% of its time waiting for weights to stream out of memory. Add the KV cache (the model's working scratchpad of past tokens), and every prompt costs real silicon time and real electricity. You aren't shipping frictionless bits; you're running a high-voltage digital bakery where every croissant costs 15 cents in butter.

2. The $600B Capex FOMO & Custom ASICs

To cope with this tax, the top hyperscalers spent ~$443B in capex (capital expenditure: upfront cash blown on land, power, and chips) in 2025, barreling past $600B in 2026.

CEOs know it's insane. Sundar Pichai acknowledged "elements of irrationality," while Mark Zuckerberg openly admitted the playbook: the risk of under-investing and missing the boat is infinitely worse than over-spending and growing into excess hardware later.

To escape NVIDIA's margin grip, everyone is building ASICs (Application-Specific Integrated Circuits: chips tailor-made for one math workload rather than general graphics):

  • Google TPU (Ironwood v7): Battle-tested, sold to external giants like Anthropic, and reportedly forcing NVIDIA to cut GPU pricing by up to 30%.
  • AWS Trainium: Over 1.4M Trainium2 chips deployed, powering most of Bedrock at a $10B+ run rate.
  • Microsoft Maia, Meta MTIA, and OpenAI's Broadcom deal: Multi-gigawatt custom silicon hedges against green-chip monopoly.

Jensen Huang dismissed custom ASICs as "noncompetitive." Of course he did—if you were selling shovels at 75% gross margins, you’d tell everyone making their own shovels is a waste of time too!

3. Consumer AI: Power Laws & The Four Seasons

At the consumer layer, weekly active users (WAU) rocketed from 100M to over 1.2 billion. But it collapsed into an extreme two-horse race:

  • ChatGPT: ~900M WAU (~70% market share). Built from ground zero without an operating system or browser default.
  • Google Gemini: ~200–250M WAU (~15–20% share), propelled by Android and Google's 4-billion-user ecosystem.
  • Everyone Else: DeepSeek, Grok, Claude, Perplexity, and Character AI share the remaining ~10%.

App store charts are deceptive. As Apoorv Agarwal highlighted, consumer AI cycles through "Four Seasons of Downloads": DeepSeek’s open-weights shock in winter, Grok’s X-fueled surge in spring, Gemini’s viral image meme in summer. Each spiked past 20M downloads and then cratered. Meanwhile, ChatGPT quietly compounded without the drama.

Downloads are first dates; weekly habits are showing up to dinner every Sunday. Attention is cheap to rent; habits are brutal to build.

4. The Retention Smile Curve

Usage tells you who tried your app; engagement tells you who stayed.

On daily stickiness (DAU:MAU), ChatGPT sits at ~45% versus Gemini’s ~22%—a 2x depth canyon. More impressively, ChatGPT's weekly stickiness (WAU:MAU) exploded from 50% in 2023 to 82% today, matching daily staples like Gmail (78%) and Instagram (92%).

Most consumer apps have retention curves that decay toward zero. ChatGPT is one of the only products on Earth (alongside Chrome and Gmail) that displays a true Smile Curve: retention drops initially, then curves back up. At Week 4, retention sits at 66% (vs. Character AI at 48%, Gemini at 44%, Claude at 41%, and Perplexity at 24%).

Why? Because ChatGPT doesn't rely on dopamine feeds or notification spam. You open it with deliberate intent to solve a problem. And every step-function upgrade—voice, canvas, reasoning, memory—reactivates lapsed users. It has crossed into becoming an indispensable cognitive utility.

5. The Attention Equation: Why Ads Outscale Subscriptions

Roughly 95% of ChatGPT users are on the free tier. $20/month subscriptions built a multi-billion-dollar foundation, but consumer internet history is unequivocal: advertising outscales subscriptions at global volume. Google's ad business is 5x Netflix; Meta is 4x Netflix.

Ad Revenue = Total Time × Ad Load × Price of Ads (CPM)

Time is the raw inventory. Total time in AI apps grew 10x over two years, and ChatGPT commands 68% of that attention (~16 mins/day per active mobile user).

Google sells keyword intent; Meta sells feed attention. But conversational AI captures structured, high-intent context: "Find a quiet hotel in Kyoto near transit for a family of four under $300." That’s why early conversational ad pilots clear $60+ CPMs (cost per 1,000 impressions).

If ChatGPT monetizes its ~850M free users at just $30/year (half of Google Search's ARPU), that’s an instant $25B advertising business.

The strategic asymmetry is sharp: Google can keep Gemini ad-free because their $295B search cash engine subsidizes the electricity. OpenAI has no legacy cash cow; they must monetize attention directly without breaking the trust of an intellectual companion.

Bottom Line

Wrestling with MS&E 435’s economic models alongside low-level systems profiling leads to three plain truths:

  1. Hardware gravity isn't lifting: Token generation is bounded by memory bandwidth and thermal physics; chipmakers will keep eating the lion's share of profits for years.
  2. Virality is vanity, habits are sanity: A 72-hour spike at #1 on the App Store means nothing without weekly retention.
  3. The winners will bridge silicon and attention: The lasting giants won't be thin prompt wrappers; they will be the platforms that co-design custom silicon to slash inference COGS while turning conversational intent into trusted monetization.

The stack will flip eventually—software always does. But it won't be next quarter. It’s going to be an arduous, decadal migration—fought token by token, cycle by cycle, and habit by habit.
